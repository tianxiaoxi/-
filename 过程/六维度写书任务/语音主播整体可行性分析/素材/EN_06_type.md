## Notes
- Voice streamer content types observed in English-speaking market:
  - **ASMR** (dominant): overwhelming majority of voice-first content. Has sub-types: traditional trigger sounds, roleplay, character-based, cosplay-ASMR
  - **Radio-Talk**: KaterFM, Elsewhere Sound Space — music-focused radio-style streaming. Very small niche on Twitch
  - **Storytelling/Interactive Fiction**: emerging — Vital Stories, serialized fiction format. Requires significant production effort
  - **Karaoke/Singing**: Ironmouse, various VTubers — voice performance through avatar
  - **Podcast**: Amouranth, StrawberryxMilky — repurposing streaming content as audio podcasts. Cross-platform strategy
  - **Voice Acting**: FredsVoice, ASMR Muzz, DrV-ASMR, Alekirser — professional actors using ASMR as a content vehicle
  - **Knowledge/Wellness**: WhispersRed — intellectual framing of ASMR as therapeutic medium
- **Type coverage gaps**: Audiobook-reading, GameVoice (live commentary without visual), Chat-Confidant (therapeutic/listening format) — these types are notably absent from search results
- The VTuber model is a critical enabler for voice-first content: provides visual anchor without requiring real face/body on camera
- **Radio-to-Twitch pipeline**: KaterFM demonstrates the college/community radio → live streaming transition
- **ASMR remains the gravitational center** of English-language voice streaming — almost all discovered voice-first creators have some ASMR element
- Multi-platform audio strategy (Twitch + Spotify + YouTube + Patreon) is common among professional voice creators
- Pure audio-only (no video, no avatar, no visual layer) streaming appears non-existent at scale — the market demands some visual component even if minimal
