Titles of related interest — THE MENTAL HEALTH INDUSTRY A Cultural Phenomenon Peter A. Magaro, Robert Gripp, & David J. McDowell This volume places the treatment of mental illness within its cultural context to illustrate how concepts of pathology arise more out of cultural beliefs than from any scientific fact. Through a well-researched review of therapy results, it shows that there is no evidence for the effectiveness of current treatment systems and examines why such research has not been performed. The book addresses the underlying issues which prevent treatment evaluation ... presents the methodological obstacles to evaluation ... offers a design for measuring the relevant variables involved in assessment ... and suggests modifications on the current treatment system. GIFT OF LIFE The Social and Psychological Impact of Organ Transplantation Roberta G. Simmons, Susan D. Klein, & Richard L. Simmons Analyzing one of the most dramatic of the new medical technologies — organ transplantation — this book covers those aspects that have general implications for public policy and sociological theory, and describes the social-psychological impact of kidney transplantation itself. As part of its focus, it examines the impact on the recipient's and donor’s self-images, and analyzes how individuals and families make decisions under stress. The final discussion concerns the health care delivery issues and the questions of funding created by the rapid rise of this new technology. THE ALIEN DOCTORS Foreign Medical Graduates in American Hospitals Rosemary Stevens, Louis Wolf Goodman, & Stephen S. Mick This original contribution to the sociology of occupations, the professions, and medicine documents the characteristics and experiences of a representative sample of physicians from all over the world who have come to this country for advanced training. Drawing upon results of the first study in this field based on a nation-wide sample, the authors examine both the role of foreign. medical graduates as manpower in the United States and their individual motivation for migration. An underlying theory of professional migration is developed. Also explored are such provocative issues as the exploitation of foreign medical graduates in United States hospitals, whether foreign medical graduates are fulfilling roles which are not expected of American physicians, and the impact of United States immigration policy.
